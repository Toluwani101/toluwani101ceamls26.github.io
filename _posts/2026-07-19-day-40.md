what_i_learned: |
  Today, we spent time training additional YOLO models that we had not yet tested with our new parameters. I also worked on creating a bar chart showing the top 15 LEGO pieces across the first seven models we trained. This chart will help us better understand which LEGO pieces were detected most consistently across different models, and we may include it in our research paper and poster to support our analysis with a clear visual representation of our results. When our faculty mentor arrived, we shifted our focus to working with the robotic arm and implementing the best model we had trained so far, which was YOLOv11m. This model had been trained on a few selected LEGO pieces, and we tested it using the camera and robotic arm to detect and pick up those pieces. The robot arm performed well overall, accurately detecting the LEGO pieces and successfully picking them up most of the time. This was an exciting step because it showed how our computer vision model could be connected to the physical robot system in a practical way. I also spent time studying the code that gives instructions to the robot arm, which helped me better understand how the software controls the robot’s movements. Later, we discussed our plans for the final presentation and what we need to complete on Monday in preparation for the results section of our research paper. Our next steps include adding the testing and validation results from our YOLOv11m model and running additional diagnostic tools, such as EigenCAM, to better understand how the model is making its predictions. Overall, today was a productive day that helped connect our model training, data analysis, and robotic implementation into one working system.
