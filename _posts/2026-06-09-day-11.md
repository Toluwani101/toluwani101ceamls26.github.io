I also learned a great deal during our third literature review session. In addition, I learned about the importance of transition words in academic writing and how different transitions should be used depending on the context, purpose, and relationship between ideas in a research paper. After the literature review session, we returned to research lab, where I gained more hands-on experience with LabelMe. We practiced drawing bounding boxes around specific objects in various images and saving the annotations for later use. I also learned how LabelMe generates annotation files that contain the coordinates of each bounding box, along with a numerical label that identifies the object's class. Seeing how image annotations are converted into data that machine learning models can understand gave me a much clearer understanding of how training datasets are prepared for object detection tasks. Overall, today helped strengthen both my research skills and my understanding of the data annotation process used in computer vision projects.
