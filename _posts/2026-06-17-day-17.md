what_i_learned: |
  Today was heavily focused on learning, testing, and evaluating the progress of our YOLO object detection model. We began by gathering and reviewing the results from the YOLO model training we completed yesterday. I also spent time learning and understanding some of the major concepts involved in YOLO performance metrics by using AI tools like ChatGPT and watching YouTube explanations. Some of the key concepts I studied included Intersection over Union (IoU), true positives, true negatives, false positives, false negatives, F1 score, precision, and recall. Learning these terms helped me better understand how object detection models are evaluated and how we can determine whether a model is performing accurately or needs improvement. We also tested some of our trained YOLO models using a webcam mounted on a tripod. The model was able to identify and label specific LEGO pieces in the images captured by the camera, which was exciting because it showed that our training process was starting to produce real results. However, we also noticed that while the model identified most LEGO pieces, it sometimes labeled objects that were not LEGO pieces, like my glasses. This showed us that there is still room to improve the accuracy of the model. Moving forward, we plan to adjust different training parameters, such as the YOLO model size, number of epochs, and other settings, to see if we can improve the model’s performance. I also began working on my end-of-week presentation slides for tomorrow, where I reflected on my accomplishments, challenges, and the progress I made throughout the week. Overall, today helped me understand that training an AI model is not just about running code, but also about evaluating results, identifying weaknesses, and making improvements through repeated testing.
