what_i_learned: |
  Today, I spent time reviewing our research paper again while our team continued training YOLO models for our project. We also began training models specifically on the top 15 LEGO piece classes that had performed the best in our previous training results. However, early in the training process, we realized that many of the top-performing classes were LEGO pieces that we did not physically have or only had in very small quantities. Because of this, we decided to shift our approach and focus on training the models with a few basic LEGO classes that we actually had available. This decision made more sense for the next stage of the project because our goal is not only to achieve strong model results, but also to successfully test and implement the model with the robotic arm using real LEGO pieces. While the models were still training, we had our SAIRI Connect session after lunch. During the session, we learned about some of the fun activities and field events planned for next week. We also selected teams for the activities and filled out forms about team colors and whether we wanted to purchase white shirts for the event. I am really looking forward to next week because it seems like it will be a great opportunity to connect more with everyone and enjoy the final part of the program. After SAIRI Connect, we returned to our research lab, checked the progress of our model training, and played Kahoot to review different concepts related to our project. We also spent time discussing our final presentation and how we want to organize our work. Finally, our mentor introduced us to CVAT, a different image annotation software that we may use for labeling images for YOLO training instead of LabelMe. Overall, today helped us make important decisions about our model training strategy, continue preparing our research paper, and begin thinking more seriously about the final presentation.

reflection: |
  Today showed me that research often requires adjusting plans based on the resources and data available. Realizing that our top-performing LEGO classes were not practical for physical testing helped me understand the importance of connecting model training decisions to real-world implementation. As we prepare for the final presentation and continue exploring tools like CVAT, I feel more aware of how important flexibility, teamwork, and documentation are in completing a successful project.
