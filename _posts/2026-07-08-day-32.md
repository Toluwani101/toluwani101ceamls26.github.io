  Today, I spent time reading documents related to our robotic arm so I could better understand the major terms, concepts, and functions involved in operating it. After reviewing the materials, my team and I also completed several Kahoot quizzes to test our knowledge and see how well we understood the information from the documents. This helped reinforce important concepts related to the robot arm and gave us a better foundation for working with the hardware side of our project. While we were learning, the YOLO model we started training yesterday was still running, although the training process had been moving very slowly. I also learned from my mentor how we could improve the accuracy of the robotic arm when picking up LEGO pieces. In order for the robot to pick up pieces precisely, we need to understand the relationship between pixels in the camera image and the actual physical distance in millimeters. To help with this, I calculated the pixel-to-millimeter ratio using different images of 2x8 LEGO pieces from our dataset. After comparing the image measurements with the real-world size of the LEGO pieces, I calculated the ratio to be approximately 2.7 pixels per 1 millimeter. This was an important step because it helps connect the camera’s image data to the robot’s physical movement, which is necessary for accurate LEGO piece detection and pickup.
