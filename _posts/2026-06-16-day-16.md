what_i_learned: |
  Today was heavily focused on problem-solving and gaining more hands-on experience with computer vision and machine learning. At the beginning of the day, I encountered a challenge while trying to convert a large LEGO image dataset that I downloaded from Kaggle. The dataset contained about 2,000 images, was approximately 15 GB in size, and included annotations stored as `.xml` files. Since YOLO requires annotations to be in `.txt` format, I had to find a way to convert all the files before I could begin training the model. Although I ran into several issues throughout the process, I was eventually able to troubleshoot and resolve them by using AI tools to guide my debugging process. After successfully converting the dataset, I learned how to properly train a large dataset using YOLOv8. We made use of an NVIDIA GPU, which significantly reduced the training time, since running the training process on our personal laptops could have taken more than 10 hours. During the training process, I learned about the importance of selecting appropriate parameters, such as the size of the YOLOv8 model, the number of training epochs, and other settings that can affect model performance. Once the initial training was complete, we documented our results and trained the model again using different parameters to compare the outcomes and hopefully improve the accuracy of the object detection system. Overall, today gave me a much deeper understanding of the iterative nature of machine learning, where testing, evaluating, and refining models are essential steps in achieving better performance.
